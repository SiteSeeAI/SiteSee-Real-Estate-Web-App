BEGIN PROMPT

Create one original, photorealistic commercial real estate image using the selected scene description. The result should have the visual quality of carefully composed, professionally photographed and edited architectural imagery prepared for a commercial property portfolio.

Depict a plausible property with coherent construction, realistic materials, and furnishings or equipment appropriate to its use. Present the building and usable space clearly. Use the supplied scene; if no scene is selected, use Image 01 from the catalogue.

FRAMING AND CROP
Output a landscape 16:9 image at the highest supported native resolution. Compose the essential subject inside a centered 4:3 area that occupies 75% of the image width and the full height. Keep the outer 12.5% on each side expendable. Fill those side areas with natural scene continuation, such as landscaping, pavement, sky, floor, or secondary wall surfaces. Keep the main building, entrance, important equipment, and dominant furniture group comfortably inside the protected area as applicable. Leave additional breathing room above and below. Both the full frame and the centered crop must feel deliberately composed. Do not draw crop guides, borders, or blank margins. For a 3840 x 2160 frame, the centered crop would be 2880 x 2160, trimming 480 pixels from each side.

ARCHITECTURAL CAMERA AND COMPOSITION
Emulate a high-quality full-frame architectural camera with a rectilinear lens and perspective control. Favor the natural spatial rendering of approximately 24-35mm full-frame focal lengths; use a wider architectural lens only when the space requires it. Select sufficient camera distance to fit the subject without stretching its proportions. Keep the horizon level and vertical building lines straight and parallel, while allowing receding lines to converge naturally. No fisheye, barrel distortion, leaning buildings, stretched corners, or exaggerated room depth. Use an appropriate standing or slightly lower interior viewpoint; use an elevated viewpoint only when the scene specifies one. Establish clear visual hierarchy, purposeful leading lines, balanced negative space, and clean separation between foreground objects. Show convincing scale, circulation, ceiling height, and the relationship between spaces. Preserve fine detail with a deep-focus, approximately f/8-f/11 photographic appearance and restrained sharpening.

LIGHTING AND FLAMBIENT
For suitable interiors, emulate the finished appearance of a carefully aligned flash-and-ambient composite. Preserve ambient light direction, soft shadows, room depth, and the character of practical fixtures. Blend subtle bounced or diffused off-camera flash for accurate surface color, controlled mixed-light casts, and gentle shadow fill. Flash should be visually unobtrusive. Preserve natural warmth where fixtures create it.

Where windows are visible, use the appearance of a restrained window exposure blend when needed. Retain plausible exterior detail and believable reflections; outdoor daylight should remain convincingly bright. Avoid dark pasted-in window views, halos around frames, flash reflections, hard flash shadows, double edges, glowing corners, and uniformly flattened illumination.

Adapt the lighting to the property. Office suites, lobbies, guestrooms, restaurants, medical spaces, and model apartments generally suit subtle flambient treatment. Warehouses, hangars, manufacturing halls, and other large or reflective spaces should retain believable ambient lighting and falloff, with localized supplemental fill only where appropriate. For exteriors and elevated site views, use natural daylight or bright overcast conditions with controlled highlights and readable shadows.

COLOR, MATERIALS, AND FINISH
Use accurate white balance, restrained saturation, natural contrast, and realistic highlight rolloff. Preserve the actual appearance of concrete, brick, glass, metal, wood, fabric, and landscaping, including subtle ordinary wear appropriate to the scene. Keep glass transparent and reflective in physically plausible ways. Maintain a consistent professional editing standard across the collection while allowing each property's materials and lighting to retain their character.

Do not use cinematic color grading, teal-and-orange treatment, dramatic sunset lighting, heavy HDR tone mapping, excessive clarity, bloom, lens flare, vignetting, crushed blacks, oversaturated skies or vegetation, plastic surfaces, or a CGI/illustration aesthetic. Avoid impossible structures, repeated or malformed objects, floating furnishings, implausible machinery, and contradictory shadows.

PRESENTATION AND OUTPUT
Keep scenes unoccupied. Include vehicles or aircraft only when the selected scene calls for them, at believable scale and subordinate to the architecture. Avoid prominent branding and decorative text. No captions, watermarks, logos, listing overlays, boundary lines, floor plans, or visible photographic equipment.

Generate one complete standalone image for the selected scene, never a collage or contact sheet. Before returning it, check the architectural geometry, lighting consistency, material realism, and centered 4:3 crop.
